Two-scene control — what actually causes B's banding

Scene 1 was a shelf of near-identical trophies — a strongly periodic texture, and a confound. So the robot was pointed at a different view (open floor, blackboard wall) and the capture repeated with the pipeline untouched. scene-dependent B amplifies whatever periodic texture the scene has

The dominant period of B's correction tracks the image exactly in both scenes — 173 px with the trophies, 253 px with the blackboards — and B multiplies it 2–4× over what Network A already produces. This is a response to image content, not to the anchors.

scene-independent B also injects a 13 px anchor lattice that A does not have

At the anchor column pitch, Network A sits at the noise floor in both scenes (≈3×) while B runs 15–23× — and it survived a complete scene change at similar strength. High-passing B−A isolates it as a uniform dot grid. This one is a genuine artefact of the re-splatted anchors.

Anchor re-splatting confirmed exactly. Scene 2 had only 703/1024 valid ToF zones — open space beyond ToF range returns nothing — and produced exactly 703 anchors, as scene 1 did at 995/995. 100% of valid zones survive in both. B's overshoot is milder here (max 6.29 m against a ToF max of 4.58 m, no 20 m clamp hit), so scene 1 was worst-case rather than typical. But B's correction went the wrong way this time — A 0.72 m → B 0.65 m against a ToF centre of 1.20 m — so its direction is not reliably toward truth.

How much of the frame the ToF actually covers

The 32×32 zone grid projects into a 447×340 px box in the middle of the 1640×1232 rectified frame — 7.5% of the image. The other 92.5% carries the affine fit but has no metric support behind it. That bounds how much of any depth map here can be believed, independently of how well either network performs.

Findings

holds Pipeline runs end-to-end on the retrained backbone

All four nodes started clean, both engines loaded, and /cloud, /depth and /depth_var published continuously at 14.1 Hz — ToF-limited as expected, no dropouts across the capture.

holds Anchor re-splatting delivers what it promised

991 of 1024 anchors (96.8%) survive to Network B's input, against roughly 5% under the old resize path. The train/deploy density mismatch that resplat_anchors targets is gone.

open Network B's far field is unbounded

B drives 0.30% of pixels past 5 m and reaches the 20 m clamp in a room the ToF measures at no more than 2.80 m. Because the residual is a log-ratio correction, a modest positive residual compounds multiplicatively wherever D₀ is already large — median ratio 1.27×, but 17.4× at the tail. Needs a range prior or a scene-aware clamp before anything downstream consumes it.

open Both networks compress the ground plane toward the camera

Down the centre column B reads 1.75 m at 20% down the frame, 0.71 m at mid-frame and 0.11 m at the bottom edge. The near floor is genuinely close — the wide rectified FOV looks steeply down and the ToF agrees, falling to 0.30 m at its bottom row — but the networks consistently under-read it: 0.11 m against a measured 0.30 m at the bottom, 0.71 m against 1.19 m at mid-frame. Worth checking the ToF↔camera extrinsic against a tape measure.

watch The uncertainty channel is saturated, and inverted

σ on /depth_var is 0.95 m median with p95 at 0.985 m — pinned near a ~1 m ceiling across a scene sitting under 3 m. Worse, σ is lowest (0.50 m) in the shelf region where B extrapolates worst, and highest (0.86 m) over the anchor-dense floor. It is most confident exactly where it is most wrong.
