Printable markers

Nineteen fiducial markers, sized from the camera's actual geometry. Each has an X whose crossing — the white dot — is the point you measure to and later click on. Print this page and the markers come out at true size.

Print at 100 % scale — not “fit to page”. Then hold a ruler against the 100 mm bar at the bottom of each sheet. If it isn’t exactly 100 mm, the print scaled and every marker is the wrong size. They will look completely normal, which is what makes this worth checking.

Why one size

An earlier version graded marker size by distance, which keeps the pixel span constant and is optically ideal — and is unusable, because markers go up before the robot is parked, so you cannot know the distance when you choose the size.

12 cm removes the decision. Pixels across = fx × size ÷ distance, with the rectified fx ≈ 380 px:

Distance0.4 m1 m 2 m3 m4 m 4.5 m
12 cm marker114 px46 px 23 px15 px11 px 10 px

Clickable across the whole range a robot on a 3.66 m field can produce — 10 px is comfortable with the capture tool’s ×8 zoom, 6 px is the floor. The four 6 cm markers on the last sheet are for surfaces a 12 cm square will not sit flat on. That is a judgement about the surface, which you can make while placing; not about distance, which you cannot.

Measuring, once they’re up

One straight-line measurement per marker, from the camera lens to the white dot — through the air, at whatever angle it runs. Not to the ToF sensor, which sits 20.2 mm lower. Not a floor or top-down distance: for a marker 1 m above the lens at 3 m ahead those differ by 162 mm, which is larger than the error being measured.
